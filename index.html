My Research

My research focuses on the modelling, analysis and calibration of dynamical systems, motivated by biological questions. I enjoy letting the biology guide the mathematics: each system calls for its own framework, whether deterministic, stochastic or hybrid. Combining mathematics and the biomedical sciences is at the heart of my work, as interdisciplinarity is of paramount importance to me.

I'm currently a PhD student in applied Mathematics in biology at UMR PCR, INRAE Centre Val de Loire and EPC-MUSCA, Inria Paris Saclay. My supervisors are Romain Yvinec (INRAE-Inria), Stefan Haar (Inria) and Frédéric Jean-Alphonse (INRAE-Inria).

My thesis has different parts:

  • Deterministic modelling: ODE models of compartmentalized cAMP signalling based on chemical reaction networks, analysing how receptor trafficking parameters shape signal dynamics and drive bistability.
  • Calibration on experimental data: fitting of the models to BRET data (cAMP biosensors) using parameter estimation and model selection.
  • Stochastic modelling: hybrid models (piecewise-deterministic Markov processes) combining continuous dynamics with discrete random events.
  • Biological application: impact of distinct cellular localizations of gonadotropin receptors on signalling compartmentalisation in the gonads.
